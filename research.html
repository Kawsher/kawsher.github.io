Research

Reliable AI for high-stakes decisions

My work sits at the intersection of deep learning, computer vision, and clinical healthcare — organized around four threads, each with its own questions, methods, and publications.

1

Evaluation & Auditing

“Benchmark accuracy is not enough.”

A model can top a leaderboard and still fail patients. My auditing work treats evaluation as a forensic discipline: stress-testing classifiers the way clinicians audit diagnoses, hunting for shortcut learning, miscalibration, and silent failure modes before a model ever reaches the bedside.

Key questions

  • How do we detect shortcut learning in chest X-ray classifiers — models that are right for the wrong reasons?
  • What does a forensic evaluation protocol look like for ensemble classifiers under real clinical distribution shift?
  • When a model collapses on external data (our locked model fell from 0.9999 to 0.6876 AUC moving across sites), how do we characterize and report that failure honestly?

Methods

Calibration analysisShortcut detectorsGrad-CAM auditingPaired bootstrap testingFailure-mode analysis

2

Generalization

“Will it work at the next hospital?”

Medical models are trained at one site and deployed at many. I work on predicting out-of-distribution performance without ever seeing target data — so a hospital can know, before deployment, whether a model will hold up on its own patients, scanners, and protocols.

Key questions

  • Can we predict a model's accuracy on unseen hospitals using only source-domain information?
  • Which signals — semantic, temporal, or statistical — actually forecast degradation under shift?
  • How do we turn a generalization forecast into a deployment decision?

Methods

OOD performance predictionSemantic augmentationDistribution-shift characterizationCross-site validation

3

Interpretability

“Show the clinician what you saw.”

Explanations are only useful if they change decisions for the better. My interpretability work focuses on human-centered explanations for clinical models: feature-level reasoning clinicians can inspect, and explanation methods audited for faithfulness rather than taken on faith.

Key questions

  • Which explanations actually help a clinician catch a model's mistake?
  • How do we verify that a saliency map reflects the model's reasoning and not an artifact of the method?
  • Can interpretable feature engineering rival black-box performance on tabular clinical data?

4

Safety & Uncertainty

“Know when to say 'I don't know.'”

The most dangerous model is a confident wrong one. I develop post-hoc uncertainty quantification for frozen, black-box vision models — no retraining, no architecture changes. By measuring spatial consistency in a model's own attention, we can flag the cases it should abstain from or refer to a clinician.

Key questions

  • How do we quantify uncertainty for a model we cannot open or retrain?
  • Can spatial consistency in attention maps serve as a reliable uncertainty signal?
  • Where should the referral threshold sit so that retained predictions are trustworthy?
