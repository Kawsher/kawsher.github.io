About Me

I am an Assistant Professor of Computer Science and Engineering at NPI University of Bangladesh, where I founded and lead the CARMA AI Lab. My research builds trustworthy AI for clinical decision-making.

My work is driven by a simple observation from the clinic: a model that is accurate on a benchmark can still fail a patient when the hospital, the scanner, or the population changes. I build methods that catch those failures before they reach the bedside.

My Path

I received my B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from the Bangladesh University of Business and Technology (BUBT) in 2021, and stayed on as a lecturer. There I mentored undergraduate research projects in medical imaging and computer vision — several of those student projects grew into peer-reviewed publications — and served as advisor to the IEEE CS BUBT student branch chapter.

I then earned my M.S. in Computer Science from Kent State University (2025), where I worked as a graduate teaching assistant while researching trustworthy machine learning for medical image analysis.

In 2026 I returned to Bangladesh to join the faculty of NPI University of Bangladesh as an Assistant Professor, where I also head the ICT Cell and founded the CARMA AI Lab.

Research

My research starts with medical imaging. I build deep learning systems for chest X-ray, MRI, and CT analysis — designed not for leaderboards, but for the clinic, where data shifts across hospitals, scanners, and patient populations. Everything else I work on serves that goal: making sure these models are reliable when it matters.

That reliability question breaks into four threads I pursue in parallel:

  • Evaluation & Auditing. Forensic auditing of ensemble classifiers: calibration, shortcut detection, and failure analysis when models meet real clinical data.
  • Generalization. Predicting out-of-distribution performance without target data. How do medical vision models behave across hospitals, scanners, and populations?
  • Interpretability. Human-centered explanations for clinical decision support: what the model looked at, and why a clinician should (or should not) trust it.
  • Safety & Uncertainty. Post-hoc uncertainty quantification for frozen black-box vision models. Knowing when to predict, when to abstain, and when to refer.

Two active threads drive this work right now: STAMP, which predicts out-of-distribution performance without target data, and SpatialUQ, which quantifies uncertainty for frozen black-box vision models from spatial consistency. Both are currently under review.
